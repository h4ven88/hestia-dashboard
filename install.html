Installing on Android

Four steps, about two minutes

You will need your hub powered on and the phone connected to the same Wi‑Fi network as the hub.

Already have Hestia installed? Uninstall it first

Only if you were given an earlier test build. This release is signed with a different key, and Android treats that as a different app, so installing over the top will simply fail.

Uninstalling clears the app's saved settings, so you will set up your hub again. That takes a few taps and nothing is typed. If your household uses a household key, have it handy.

Download and open the file

Tap Download APK above. Your browser will warn that this type of file can harm your phone, which it says for every APK. Open the file once it finishes.

Android will then say installs from that source are not allowed, and offer Settings. It means whichever app opens the file, which is not always the one that downloaded it: open it from My Files on a Samsung phone and it is My Files that needs the permission, not the browser. Grant it to whatever the dialog names. That permission applies only to that one app, not to your phone generally.

Let Play Protect scan it

Google offers to check the app before it installs. Say yes. It scanned clean on the phones this has been installed on, and it takes a few seconds.

Depending on your device you may instead see wording about an unknown developer, which is Android noting the app did not come from the Play Store rather than reporting anything found wrong with it. Choose More details, then Install anyway.

Open Hestia and confirm your hub

The app explains what it is about to do, asks permission to find devices on your network, then searches. When it finds your hub it shows you the hub's name and its certificate fingerprint so you can confirm it is yours before anything is trusted.

If you decline the network permission, discovery cannot work and the app will tell you so. Android remembers that choice, so you may need to re-enable it in your phone's app settings.

Before you start

What works, and what does not yet

This is a beta and it is worth being plain about the edges. None of the items on the right are faults you need to report. They are known, and some are not fixable from our side.

What the app adds

  • It finds your hub. No address to type, no certificate to accept by hand.
  • Notifications with the app closed. Door, motion, lock, smoke, water and alarm alerts.
  • Camera snapshots served through your hub now work, which a browser blocks.
  • Nothing loaded from the internet. Every asset ships inside the app; it talks only to your hub and your own settings sync.

Known limits

  • Fire tablets cannot run this. Fire OS has no Google Play Services, so notifications cannot work at all. Use the browser version there.
  • Cameras with their own IP address, UniFi and similar, are not supported in the app yet. Hub‑served snapshots are.
  • Force‑stopping the app stops notifications until you open it again. That is Android's rule for apps you explicitly stop, and nothing in the app can change it.
  • Outside the US, Google began enforcing developer verification for directly‑installed apps in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand on 30 September 2026. In those countries this file may be blocked.
Browser support

Most browsers work. A few have quirks.

The dashboard itself works anywhere. What varies is voice and notifications, because those depend on browser features that are not evenly implemented.

Chrome, Edge and Brave: fully supported

Everything works, including voice and notifications. On Brave, notifications need “Use Google services for push messaging” turned on in Brave's own settings, otherwise registering for them fails with no useful error.

Firefox: fully supported

No known issues.

Safari and iOS: partial

The dashboard works. Voice input does not, because Safari records audio in a format the speech service does not accept. Notifications behave differently from other browsers and are not something we can promise there yet. Both are reasons the iOS app is on the roadmap.

Samsung Internet: known display issue

Colours can render incorrectly. This is most likely Samsung Internet's own dark-mode setting rewriting the page's colours on top of a theme that is already dark. If the dashboard looks wrong there, try Chrome before reporting it.

Fire tablets and kiosk browsers: dashboard only

The dashboard works, but voice does not: the Fire OS browser engine lacks the audio recording API it needs. Notifications are unavailable too, since Fire OS has no Google Play Services. Fully Kiosk users with a self-signed hub certificate may also need to enable Ignore SSL Errors in its advanced web settings.

A Play Store release is planned, so the app can update itself and install without warnings. It is on the roadmap along with an iOS app and support for cameras on their own address.
