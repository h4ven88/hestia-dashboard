# v2.2.0 is out, and it is a security release

Two of you reported cloud sync problems this week, and chasing them turned up
something considerably worse than the problem you reported. So this release is
mostly security, plus the fixes for what you actually hit.

**Update both halves:** re-paste `HestiaDashboard.groovy`, then open the app and
click **⬇ Update Dashboard File**. You do not need to click Done this time.

## The one that matters

Hestia stores your settings in the cloud keyed by your public IP address. If your
ISP puts you behind shared addressing, which a lot of them do, other households
share that key with you.

Two endpoints had no authentication. One handed out a list of your sensors,
including your smoke detectors and their device ids. The other accepted device
events for those ids without checking who was sending them. And smoke alerts
deliberately bypass the armed check, because a fire matters whether or not you
armed the house.

Chained together, somebody sharing your public address could make every phone in
your house say **"Smoke / CO Alert"**, at three in the morning, as often as they
liked. They could also read a timeline of when your doors opened and when you
disarmed, add their own phone to your notifications, or quietly delete your
devices so your alarms stopped arriving.

All of that is closed. No evidence any of it was ever used, but it was possible,
and that is enough.

## What you reported

**Your household could re-key itself and lock you out.** A device that could not
reach the companion app invented its own household key. That is not an edge case:
it happens routinely on hestari.com, because a browser cannot discover the
companion app across origins. One device would re-encrypt the shared record and
every other device lost access to its own settings. Three separate guards now
stop that, including one in the hub app itself that catches it even from older
dashboards.

**You can now enter a household key on a device that still works.** Before, the
only place to type one was the first-run recovery screen, which never appears on
a device that already has settings. Diagnostics told you to use it anyway, and
the only way to reach it was to wipe the device and start over, which created a
new key and broke everything else. It is now in Settings → Diagnostics, and it
checks the key against your real record before saving it.

**Regenerating your Maker API token no longer locks you out forever.** It used to,
permanently, because that token was the only thing proving you owned the record.
There is now a second route that survives it.

**Diagnostics stopped lying to you.** If you open the dashboard from your hub's
address, cloud features cannot work from there, and it used to report that as
"could not reach hestari.com" when it had never asked hestari.com anything. It
now names the real reason, and it also says what is still fine: your hub keeps
firing reminders, and push still reaches devices already registered.

## One thing to be aware of

If a rejected write warns you that notifications are being refused, note that
**alarm and arm/disarm notifications stop while door, window and motion ones keep
arriving**. That combination looks like a working system and is not. Diagnostics
now says so explicitly.

## Changeover

Only a browser can register the new webhook, so the old unauthenticated form is
still accepted **until 2026-11-30** rather than cutting off households that do
not open their dashboard often. Your household switches over by itself the first
time you open the dashboard after updating. If you have updated and not opened
it, open it once.

## Honest caveat

If your household is *already* locked out after regenerating a Maker API token,
this release does not retroactively let you back in. The new route did not exist
when your record was written. Your options are the original token if you still
have it, or waiting for the record to lapse, and Diagnostics will now tell you
the date.
