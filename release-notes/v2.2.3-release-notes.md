# Hestia v2.2.3 Release Notes

**Release Date:** 2026-10-24

The Android app is installable, and the banner that asks you to update your
companion app now takes you straight to it.

---

## How to update

1. Copy `HestiaDashboard.groovy`, open **Apps Code**, replace the existing
   code, and **Save**.
2. Open **Apps → Hestia Dashboard** and click **⬇ Update Dashboard File**.

**Done is not required.** `initialize()` is unchanged, so there is no new
scheduled job or subscription to register. The Groovy is byte-identical to
v2.2.2 apart from its two version strings, which move in lockstep so the
companion-app check in Diagnostics does not report a mismatch.

---

## The Android app

**hestari.com/install.** Android 8.0 or later, phones and tablets.

It is not a wrapper around the website. It finds your hub itself over mDNS,
shows you the hub's certificate fingerprint and pins that exact key, and
relays every hub request over that pinned connection. Nothing is typed during
setup: no address, no Maker API token, no app ID.

What it changes, in practice:

- **No certificate interstitial on every device.** Instead of a browser
  warning you click through, you are shown the hub's actual fingerprint once
  and asked to trust it. If that key ever changes, you hear about it before
  anything connects.
- **Notifications arrive with the app closed.** Not backgrounded, closed.
  Android can kill the process entirely and a push still starts it: measured
  at 28 seconds from a dead process after eight hours of deep sleep.
- **Hub camera snapshots work.** Snapshots that come through the hub are
  fetched over the pinned connection, so there is no certificate to accept and
  no mixed content to argue with.

Three defects were found by running the release build on a wiped phone rather
than by reading it: first contact with a hub could time out and report "No
answer from that address" on a hub that was answering; the device poll built a
new HTTP client per request and so did a full TLS handshake roughly once a
second; and a failure inside the native bridge was logged nowhere at all. All
three are fixed.

**Known limits.** Fire tablets cannot run it, as Fire OS has no Play Services.
Camera snapshots served from a camera's own address rather than through the hub
are not supported yet. A force-stopped app receives nothing until reopened,
which is Android's own rule.

**It has been tested on one phone, one household, one Android skin.** That is
the honest state of it.

## The update banner has somewhere to go

When the companion app on your hub is out of date, Hestia says so. Until now
that was all it did, which left you to open Hubitat, find Apps, and hunt for
Hestia yourself.

The banner now carries **Open your Hestia app**, linking straight to the app's
own page on your hub, where the update button already lives.

The path was checked against a real hub rather than assumed. It degrades to
plain text if the hub address or the app's id is not known yet, so it never
offers a link that goes nowhere.

---

## About v2.2.2

If you saw v2.2.2 go past and wondered what it was: a minor edit. Site and
layout corrections, with one fix worth naming for app users.

- The hub-unreachable banner used to render as three overlapping blocks of
  text on a phone. It is a sentence now and lays out as one.
- In the app, that banner used to say "open your hub's address and accept the
  certificate", which is impossible in an app: a WebView offers no way to
  accept one, which is precisely why the app pins the key during setup
  instead. App users now get advice they can act on.
- The landing page no longer slides sideways on a phone, and an install page
  was added at `/install`.

Nothing in v2.2.2 required action on your hub. If you skipped it, updating to
v2.2.3 brings you everything in both.
