# Hestia v2.0.0: Cloud sync gets a real trust model

This one is a major version because the way your synced settings are stored changed at the foundation. Using Hestia doesn't change at all.

**One thing is required: re-paste the companion app.** `HestiaDashboard.groovy` from this release, into the Hestia app code in Hubitat, save. The app is what hands your household secret to your devices, so it needs this version. Everything else carries over on its own.

## What was wrong

If you use Cloud Sync, your settings are stored under a key derived from your home's public IP. The encryption key was derived from **that same IP plus a fixed string sitting in Hestia's own source code**, which anyone can read, because this project is open source.

So anyone leaving the internet from the same public IP as you could work out your key and read your record: Maker API token, both PIN hashes, camera URLs, Anthropic and Google keys.

If your house has its own public IP, that means someone inside your house, and the practical risk was low. **If your ISP has you behind CGNAT, you share that IP with a lot of unrelated homes**, and any of them could have done it. I have no reason to think anyone did. It was still true and it shouldn't have been, so here's the fix rather than a quiet patch note.

## What it is now

Your household gets its own 256-bit secret, generated on your own device. It never goes to hestari.com. Keys come off it via HKDF-SHA256, and the record is AES-GCM-256.

The record is split. The **private half** holds everything sensitive and **the servers have no way to decrypt it**. Not "aren't supposed to", there's no decryption code, because a backend that could still read it all would make the split decorative.

The **service half** is readable on purpose and holds only what the servers need to answer "a door opened, should anyone be notified?". Notification settings, the IDs and names of the sensors and locks involved, and a hash of your Maker token. No tokens, no keys, no PINs, no camera URLs. There's a test suite whose only job is to fail if anything else ever creeps into that list.

Writes now have to prove they hold your Maker API token, so a neighbour on your IP can't overwrite your household's record any more.

**Worth stating plainly: your raw Maker token is now sent to hestari.com on every settings save.** Over HTTPS, hashed, compared, discarded. It's never stored, and the record only ever holds the hash. If you don't want that trade, turn Cloud Sync off and Hestia runs entirely against your hub. Rotating your token won't lock you out, there's a separate recovery path for that.

## Pairing is nothing

No code to type, no QR to scan. **Your hub distributes the secret**, through the companion app's config store, next to the config your devices already pull from it. A new phone or tablet on your network picks it up the same way it picks up everything else. The Wall Panel URL carries it too.

Existing households migrate themselves on first contact, with no gap where notifications stop. A device that somehow has no secret keeps working against your hub rather than falling back to the old weak scheme.

## A real screen when the connection drops

Losing the hub used to give you a red bar at the top, which is easy to miss on a tablet across the room. After twenty seconds you now get an actual screen: what went wrong, a refresh button in the middle, the gem. It names the likely cause for your setup instead of guessing. It stays out of the way during an alarm or an entry countdown.

## Also

Diagnostics' Cloud Sync and Push checks now do a real round trip instead of trusting local state, so a device that's quietly stopped being registered will show up as failing. The companion app also protects your household secret from being erased by a browser tab still running an older version.

From here the companion app gets re-pasted on every version bump and its version will always match the dashboard's. It has drifted behind before, which made it hard to tell whether an update had actually landed.
