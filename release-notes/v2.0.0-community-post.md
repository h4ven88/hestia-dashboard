# Hestia v2.0.0: Cloud sync gets a real trust model

Major version because the way your synced settings are stored changed at the foundation. Using Hestia doesn't change at all, and your settings, devices, rooms, PINs and notifications all carry over untouched.

## What you need to do

**Update the companion app.** Either HPM (**Update**, tick **Hestia Dashboard**), or paste [`HestiaDashboard.groovy`](https://github.com/h4ven88/hestia-dashboard/blob/main/HestiaDashboard.groovy) over **Apps Code → Hestia Dashboard** and **Save**.

**Then open the Hestia app and click ⬇ Update Dashboard File.** Easy to miss and it matters: saving the code in Apps Code does not pull the new dashboard down to your hub. That button does, and it forces the download every time. Do not rely on Done for this, it runs a conditional download that skips when it thinks you are current and gives up silently if that check times out.

The dashboard itself needs nothing. On hestari.com just reload. Served from your hub, it arrives when you click Update Dashboard File above, or within three hours on its own.

**Check it:** Settings → Diagnostics → Run all checks. **Companion App** green "v2.0.0 (up to date)" means both halves agree. If **Cloud Sync** says "Not migrated yet", that's expected right after updating, not a fault. Reload once and it goes green.

Worth doing promptly if you run a wall panel. Your devices won't update together, and a device left on the old dashboard can wipe the household secret for the others. The new app prevents that.

## What was wrong

If you use Cloud Sync, your settings were encrypted with a key derived from your home's public IP plus **a fixed string sitting in this project's own source code**, which anyone can read.

So anyone leaving the internet from the same public IP as you could work out your key and read your record: Maker API token, both PIN hashes, camera URLs, Anthropic and Google keys.

If your house has its own public IP, that means someone inside your house, and the real risk was low. **If your ISP has you behind CGNAT, you share that IP with a lot of unrelated homes.** I have no reason to think anyone did this. It was still true and it shouldn't have been.

## What it is now

Your household gets its own 256-bit secret, generated on your device and never sent to hestari.com. The record is split: the sensitive half is encrypted and **the servers hold no decryption code at all**, because a backend that could still read it would make the split decorative. The readable half holds only what's needed to answer "a door opened, should anyone be notified?" — no tokens, no keys, no PINs, no camera URLs.

Writes now have to prove they hold your Maker API token, so a neighbour on your IP can't overwrite your record.

**Stating this plainly: your raw Maker token is now sent to hestari.com on every settings save.** Over HTTPS, hashed, compared, discarded, never stored. If you'd rather not, turn Cloud Sync off and Hestia runs entirely against your hub. Rotating your token won't lock you out.

Nothing to pair. Your hub hands the secret to your devices alongside the config they already pull from it, and existing households migrate themselves with no gap in notifications.

## Also

Losing the hub now gives you an actual screen after twenty seconds, naming the likely cause, instead of a red bar that's easy to miss on a tablet across the room. It stays out of the way during an alarm or entry countdown. Diagnostics' Cloud Sync and Push checks now do a real round trip instead of trusting local state, so a device that's quietly stopped being registered shows up as failing.
