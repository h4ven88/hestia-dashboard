# Hestia v2.1.2 Release Notes

**Release Date:** 2026-09-30

If you updated to v2.1.0 or v2.1.1 and your reminders never fired, this is why, and this fixes it.

---

## Reminders were not firing at all

**Start here: open the Hestia app on your hub and click Done.** That alone fixes it on your hub right now, with or without this release.

The companion app registers its once-a-minute reminder job inside `initialize()`, and Hubitat runs that only when an app is installed or updated. Updating means **opening the app and clicking Done**. Pasting new code into Apps Code and saving does not do it. Neither does "⬇ Update Dashboard File".

The v2.1.0 update instructions told everyone to click Update Dashboard File and specifically *not* Done. Those instructions were mine and they were wrong for that release, because that release was the one that added the job. So on any hub that followed them the job was never scheduled.

The hub then reported the correct version, answered every request correctly, and held a perfectly good list of what was due. It simply never looked at it.

---

## The instructions were the trigger. The design was the bug.

The dashboard decided the hub was handling reminders based on the companion app's **version number**. A version number tells you what is installed. It cannot tell you what is running.

So a hub with an unregistered job looked exactly like a healthy one. Every open dashboard stood down and waited for a hub that was never going to act, and the hub had nothing waiting for it. Both sides silent, and nothing on any screen to say so.

**The hub now has to prove it is running, not just installed.** Its reminder job reports a heartbeat, and the dashboard checks it. No heartbeat, or a stale one, and your screens fire reminders themselves, exactly as they do for households with no companion app. Freshness is judged against your hub's own clock, never the device's, so a wall panel with a drifting clock cannot wrongly declare a healthy hub dead.

**And the hub repairs itself.** If its reminder job has not run in over eleven minutes, it re-registers it. That happens both when a dashboard checks in and on the app's own three-hour cycle, so a household that relies on pushed reminders and keeps no screen open recovers too. If it genuinely cannot be repaired it stops after three attempts and says so plainly in the hub log, rather than retrying forever.

**Settings → Reminders now tells you which side is firing them** — "Fired by your hub" or "Fired by this device" — with what to do about it. That was invisible before, and it is the whole difference between a reminder that reaches you when everything is closed and one that does not.

---

## Updating

1. **Companion app.** HPM, or paste `HestiaDashboard.groovy` over Apps Code → Hestia Dashboard and Save.
2. **Open the Hestia app and click Done.** Required this time, not optional. Done is the only thing that re-registers the reminder job.
3. **Then click ⬇ Update Dashboard File** to pull the new dashboard to your hub.
4. **The dashboard.** On hestari.com, reload. The footer should read v2.1.2.
5. **Check it.** In the app's status page, Scheduled Jobs should list `reminderTick`. Settings → Reminders should say "Fired by your hub".

---

## For the technically inclined

The heartbeat is written by the tick itself, throttled to once every five minutes and recorded **before** any early return, so a household with no reminders yet still reports a live job rather than one that merely had nothing to do.

The repair is rate limited to one attempt every three minutes, and that limit is load-bearing rather than tidiness. `runEvery1Minute` schedules at a randomised position within the interval and replaces any existing schedule, and the dashboard polls roughly every five seconds. Repairing on every poll would cancel and recreate the job around twelve times a minute, and if that randomised position is re-drawn each time, the job would rarely survive long enough to run. Whether it is re-drawn is not documented and could not be established, so the interval is set well beyond the job's own period to make the question moot.

There is also a two-minute grace period after the app initialises. The job is registered immediately but its heartbeat does not exist until it first runs, up to a minute later, so without that window every fresh install would have produced a burst of warnings about a job that had just been registered and was working fine.

Not claimed, deliberately: that this repairs a scheduler stuck for other reasons. Those community reports trace to database corruption and were resolved by a database reset, not by re-registering a job. The three-attempt ceiling exists so that case ends in one clear error instead of endless churn on a hub that is already unwell.

Known limitation: the repair restores the reminder job but not the restart handler, so after a hub reboot an occurrence missed during downtime is picked up by the next minute's pass rather than immediately. Up to a minute of delay, never a lost reminder.

494 tests, and fourteen deliberate breaks of this code, all caught. The Groovy half has no execution environment anywhere in this project's toolchain and was reviewed against Hubitat's documentation instead; that review found three real defects in the first version of this fix, all corrected before release.
