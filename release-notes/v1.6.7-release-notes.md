# Hestia v1.6.7 Release Notes

**Release Date:** 2026-09-14

---

## The entry-delay countdown now actually appears

When your system is armed and a door opens, your alarm gives you a window to disarm before it goes off. Artemis was supposed to show that countdown, open the PIN pad, and speak a warning. **It never did.** Arming worked, disarming worked, and the exit countdown after tapping Arm Away worked, but the moment that mattered most, walking in to an armed house, showed nothing.

## Why

Everything that reacted to your alarm system in real time (the entry-delay countdown, the full-screen alarm when a countdown expires, and the spoken arm/disarm announcements) lived in one piece of code that listened for live events from the hub. That listener pointed at an address Hubitat does not provide, and nothing in Hestia ever started it. It had been silently inactive since it was written. The exit countdown only appeared to work because tapping Arm Away starts it locally, without waiting to hear from the hub.

## What changed

The countdown is now driven by **what your alarm system reports**, not by guessing from a sensor. Only the alarm system knows whether that door is in an armed zone and whether a delay applies, so Hestia no longer tries to decide that itself.

- **Ring:** Hestia reads the entry delay, exit delay, and siren state straight from your Ring base station through Maker API. Ring reports its own countdown, so the ring on screen matches the seconds on your keypad.
- **Hubitat Safety Monitor:** HSM's entry delay is not something Maker API can see, so the Hestia companion app on your hub now records HSM's alerts and passes them to the dashboard.
- **Both feed the same countdown.** Whichever system reports the delay first starts it. One system reporting "no delay" will not cancel a countdown the other one is running.

When a delay starts, Hestia opens Security, shows the countdown, opens the PIN pad on phones, and announces it on devices with Announcements enabled. If the countdown runs out and the siren sounds, it switches to the full alarm screen. Disarming from anywhere (the Hestia PIN pad, your Ring keypad, the Ring app, or a rule) closes it.

## Official and community Ring integrations

Hestia now recognizes both.

- The community Ring integration's **Ring Virtual Alarm Hub** is recognized as an alarm panel for the first time. It reports delays differently from the official integration, and Hestia previously ignored it entirely.
- If you have both installed, Hestia watches both for delays and sends commands to whichever one is actually connected to your alarm. Previously it always picked the official base station, even on hubs where that integration can't reach the panel, so every command sent to Ring there failed quietly.

## You no longer need rules to keep HSM and Ring in sync

Keeping Hubitat Safety Monitor and a Ring alarm in step has usually meant building Rule Machine rules. Hestia now does it for you: **Arm Away, Arm Home, and Disarm in Hestia go to both HSM and Ring at the same moment.** One tap and both systems match.

- If you already sync them with rules, nothing breaks. Your rule sends the same command Hestia already sent, and Ring ignores a request for the mode it's already in.
- Disarm is always sent to Ring, even when Hestia believes Ring is already disarmed, because entering your PIN has to stop the alarm you're hearing.
- This covers changes made in Hestia. Arming or disarming from the Ring keypad or Ring app is outside Hestia's view while no screen is open, so if you want those mirrored to HSM, turn on the community Ring driver's own "sync Ring to HSM" option rather than building a rule.

## Push notifications no longer cry wolf during an entry delay

v1.6.5 was supposed to make the "Security" push say "Alarming in 60 seconds, disarm to cancel" while the delay runs, and save "intrusion alarm triggered" for when it actually goes off. It checked for the wrong word. HSM labels its entry-delay alert `intrusion-home-delay`; the word "pending" only appears in HSM's log text. So walking in to an armed house still pushed "intrusion alarm triggered!" to every phone immediately. Fixed, and Night mode is now named correctly in the message instead of being reported as Away.

## Camera refresh interval

**Settings → Cameras → Refresh every** sets how often camera snapshots update: 1, 2, 5, 10, 30, or 60 seconds. The same interval applies in the camera grid and when you enlarge a camera, and it defaults to 5 seconds. Previously the grid updated every 30 seconds with no way to change it.

- Cameras with a Live Stream URL keep playing live (about one frame a second) when enlarged, whatever the setting. A live stream isn't a snapshot, so it doesn't slow down with the grid.
- Snapshot-only cameras now refresh in the enlarged view too. Before, only cameras with a live stream URL did; a snapshot camera froze on whatever frame was showing when you opened it.
- Images update in place, so tiles no longer flash blank on each refresh, and a slow camera is given time to finish sending its frame instead of being cut off.
- A camera that's offline no longer triggers a full redraw and a network check on every failed attempt.

## HTTPS hub URL no longer reverts to HTTP

If you set your hub URL to `https://<hub-ip>` (no port), Hestia quietly changed it back to `http://` every time it loaded your settings. On a page loaded over HTTPS that meant "Hub unreachable" after every refresh, even though the change seemed to work until then. That rewrite was based on the belief that hubs don't serve HTTPS on the standard port. They do.

When Hestia is loaded from your hub itself (`/local/index.html`), it now connects back to the hub exactly the way that page was loaded. That's the one address guaranteed to work in that browser, because it already accepted it to show the page. Your hub address is still shared with your other devices, and this per-device choice is not, so one device's setting can't break another.

**Using HTTPS with the hub on more than one device?** Each phone, tablet, and computer has to accept the hub's certificate once: open `https://<hub-ip>` in that device's browser and continue past the warning, then open Hestia.

## Reconnecting a reinstalled companion app

When the Hestia app on your hub is reinstalled, it gets a new app ID. A dashboard loaded from your hub finds the new ID by itself, but one loaded from hestari.com can't: browsers don't let it read the hub's discovery file. Diagnostics used to tell you to open the dashboard from your hub's address once so the new ID would carry over. That could never work, because the hand-off relies on cloud sync, which doesn't run from a page loaded from the hub.

Settings → Diagnostics now shows **Reconnect the companion app** whenever the Companion App check fails on a device that isn't loaded from the hub. It links to your hub's discovery file; copy that page, paste it in, and click Reconnect. Hestia checks the details with your hub before saving anything, refuses a file from a different hub, and once it works, the new ID reaches your other devices on its own.

## Also fixed

- **Spoken arm and disarm announcements** ("Armed away," "Disarmed," "Arming away, 60 seconds starting now") now play. They were part of the same inactive code.
- The countdown ring no longer restarts from full when the screen redraws partway through.
- A disarm from somewhere other than this screen now clears an intrusion alarm screen and closes an open PIN pad.
- Hestia will not auto-refresh the page while a countdown or alarm is on screen.

## Setup

- **Ring users: make sure your alarm base station is selected in Maker API.** That's Apps → Maker API → select devices. For the community integration, the device is **Ring Virtual Alarm Hub**; selecting only its sensors isn't enough. The alarm base station has no tile on your dashboard, so it's easy to miss.
- **This release includes Groovy changes.** Update via Hubitat Package Manager, or re-paste `HestiaDashboard.groovy` under Apps Code, then **open the Hestia app on your hub and click Done.** The HSM countdown and the push notification fix both need it. Until your hub is on v1.6.7, the HSM half stays switched off on its own; the Ring half works without it.

## Notes

- Your Rule Machine rules that keep HSM and Ring in sync are unaffected.
- The companion app logs each HSM alert it receives (`Hestia: hsmAlert value=...`). If the countdown doesn't appear on an HSM-only setup, that log line is the first thing to check.
- The HSM countdown uses the Entry Delay you set in Hestia's Artemis settings, because HSM doesn't report its own delay length. Keep the two matched. Ring reports its own, so Ring users don't need to.
